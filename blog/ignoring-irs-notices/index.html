What Happens If You Ignore IRS Notices About Tax Debt?

The Escalating Consequences of Ignoring IRS Notices

Nobody likes getting mail from the IRS. However, burying your head in the sand and ignoring the notices can quickly land you in trouble. From accelerated IRS enforcement to compound penalties stacking up, this move leads to consequences you’d rather avoid. Yet, this is one of the most common mistakes taxpayers make.

It’s important to educate yourself on the potential consequences of ignoring notices. In the following guide, we explain the implications of failing to respond swiftly to IRS tax notices.

Key Takeaways

  • IRS communication escalates in stages, from minor correction letters to levy warnings.
  • Ignoring notices accelerates enforcement and compounds penalties.
  • Failure to respond can cause you to lose important appeal rights, such as the ability to request a Collection Due Process hearing.
  • Enforcement tools can include wage garnishments, bank levies, federal tax liens, and, in rare cases, asset seizure.
  • Proactive communication, or professional help, reduces the risk of enforcement.
 

Spectrum of IRS Communication: Not All Silence is Equally Risky

Not all IRS communication is created equal. The first step in understanding your risk is to determine which notice you’re dealing with. Below, we break down the different levels: 
  • Return adjustment notices. CP12 or CP2000 notices often ask you to verify income or confirm minor discrepancies on your tax return. If you answer these promptly, you can prevent small issues from snowballing. Ignoring them, however, opens the door to larger penalties or adjustments being made without your input.
  • Audit notices. An audit letter, such as Letter 566 or 525, requires you to provide documentation. If you ignore it, the IRS decides how to make changes to your return, based solely on what it has on record, often inflating what you owe. 
  • Unfiled return notices. Similarly, failure to respond to unfiled tax return notices like CP59 allows the IRS to file a substitute return for you and start the collection process.
  • Early IRS collection notices. Letters like CP501, CP503, and CP504 are demands for payment. Ignoring these demands signals to the IRS that you are unwilling to cooperate, which raises the risk of enforcement. As notices escalate, the tone becomes sharper. 
  • Urgent IRS notices. A Final Notice of Intent to Levy, such as Letter 1058 or LT11, means that the IRS has moved from asking to warning. Failure to respond means the IRS can garnish wages, levy bank accounts (including joint accounts), and begin seizing assets.

 As you can see, the agency may contact you about a small discrepancy with your return – if you don't respond, they'll move forward with the adjustment, and you'll owe tax. Then, they'll start adding interest and penalties, while sending demands for payment. If you continue to ignore the IRS, they'll start garnishing wages or seizing your assets.

The Domino Effect: How Silence Triggers IRS Action

Taxpayers often make the mistake of presuming they can ignore these notices. However, every ignored notice accelerates the problem. Here are the issues you may face:  

Increased tax debt

You will quickly find that penalties for failing to file or pay start stacking from day one. Interest also gets added to the debt. Penalties can get up to 50% of your original tax debt, and with interest compounding daily, the debt can snowball quickly.  

Limited negotiation options

It doesn’t end there. Failing to respond to notices limits your options. You may lose your appeal rights, and depending on the specifics, you may not be eligible for payment plans, Offers in Compromise, and even temporary hardship relief if you don’t respond early. Waiting until the IRS initiates enforcement makes negotiations much harder.  

Loss of rights 

Perhaps the most damaging consequence is the loss of appeal rights. For example, a Collection Due Process hearing must be requested within 30 days of a Final Notice of Intent to Levy. If you miss that window, you give up a strong legal defense that could have paused IRS action.   

Severe enforcement action

Once the IRS moves into the enforcement stage, the consequences are severe. For instance, wage garnishments can consume a large portion of your paycheck, leaving you with little to cover living expenses. Bank levies literally drain accounts with little warning, which creates immediate financial stress. Federal tax liens limit your ability to borrow or sell property. In the most extreme situations, the IRS can also seize your assets outright.  

Breaking the Silence: Steps to Take When a Notice Arrives

If you have received notices from the IRS, it’s time to break your silence. We advise taking proactive steps to comply with the IRS and lower your risk of any enforcement action. Consider these tips:

Step one: Read the notice thoroughly 

The first step is simple but vital: open and read the notice. You need to understand why it was sent, what action the IRS expects you to take, and the deadline for a response. Even if you don’t fully understand the letter, acknowledging it puts you in a stronger position than ignoring it.   Should you need help navigating the letter, reach out to a trusted tax attorney. An expert will be able to help you understand any legal jargon and get to grips with the next steps.   

Step two: Understand the requirements 

Once you know what the IRS is asking, you can figure out what action you need to take. This depends on the type of notice you’re dealing with.    Sometimes it may be as simple as correcting a mistake or confirming certain information. Other times you’ll need to pay a balance, file a return, or prepare for an audit. If anything is unclear, don’t hesitate to call the IRS directly using the phone number provided on the notice.  

Step three: Keep your own records 

When you’re resolving the problem, you need to keep detailed records of everything. That means saving the notice, keeping records of dates of communication, and documenting any payments or agreements. This paper trail is invaluable if questions or disputes arise later.  

Step four: Explore resolution options 

The final step is about finding the right resolution for you. The IRS offers various options depending on your circumstances. These include payment plans, settlement opportunities through an Offer in Compromise, and hardship status known as Currently Not Collectible. 

Knowing which route fits your financial situation can make the difference between a manageable resolution and having to deal with overwhelming enforcement action. At Nashville Tax Solutions, we can easily help you determine the best option for your tax situation.

FAQs

What’s the worst that can happen if I ignore an IRS notice?

If you continually ignore IRS notices, it can lead to enforcement action. This includes wage garnishment, bank levies, federal tax liens, and even asset seizure.  

How do I know if an IRS notice is fake?

The IRS will always communicate by mail at first. Scammers, on the other hand, may call you or email you directly. If you’re worried that a notice is fake, a tax professional can help you.  

Do all IRS notices mean I owe money?

Not necessarily. Some IRS notices simply require you to give some information or clarification on your status. Of course, others highlight discrepancies. Not every letter demands payment.    It’s important to read your notice thoroughly and make sure you understand what it means. Working with a tax professional can help you if you’re unsure of how to proceed.   

How long do I have to respond before enforcement begins?

That depends on the notice in question. Make sure you read it all the way through to find out what the next steps are and what deadlines you have to adhere to. Final Notices of Intent to Levy usually give you 30 days to act before collection begins.  

Can the IRS take my paycheck or bank account if I ignore a notice?

Yes. If you ignore notices, wage garnishments and bank levies are some of the first actions the IRS may take.  

What should I do if I’ve already ignored notices?

If you’ve ignored previous notices, don’t delay things any further. Contact us at Nashville Tax Solutions, and we will help you determine what the next steps should be.
